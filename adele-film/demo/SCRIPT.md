# Adele Care demo film: "One week at Riverside Dental" (script v4, about 4:30)

For dentists and practice owners, to share instead of explaining Adele every time. Narrated by Adele in the
first person (the voice of the 90-second film). No captions.

**The story.** We follow one practice through one week. Act 1: the phone rings at 8am with nobody there to
answer, and every gap in the week costs a chair. Act 2: Monday to Friday, each day brings a real problem and
Adele solves it in front of us, while a "Chairs filled this week" counter in the corner keeps climbing.
Act 3: Friday at five, the owner opens the weekly report, sees what the week was worth, and stays in control.

**Messages.** More revenue (recall, cancellations filled, confirmed appointments). WhatsApp first: where
patients are, and cheaper than SMS for reminders. You stay in control, and your team only handles what needs
a person. Full NHS compliance and cybersecurity protocols are planned.

**Sources.** Every capability comes from the Adele Care brochure and the "Adele Care Phase 1 prototype"
specification. Names, times and counts are illustrative (the counter is labelled "Example week"). No £ figures.

## Act 1: the empty reception (0:00-0:32)

**1. Cold open (0:00-0:12).** Dim reception, chairs empty, a desk phone ringing. On screen: "Monday 8:00 ·
Reception opens 8:30". The ring keeps going.

> It's eight o'clock on Monday morning. Reception doesn't open for half an hour, and the phone is already ringing.

**2. What it costs (0:12-0:32).** The ring cuts to silence. A missed call, a cancelled 2pm and an overdue
recall list appear one by one and drift into an empty chair. Then the Adele symbol lights the screen.

> Every unanswered call, every cancellation left empty, every recall nobody had time to send, is an empty
> chair. Your team can't be everywhere. I can. I'm Adele, the AI receptionist for UK dental practices, and
> this is one week at Riverside Dental.

## Act 2: the week (0:32-3:46)

**3. How I work (0:32-0:48).** Phone, WhatsApp, SMS, email and web chat flow into Adele ("Practice knowledge
base · your booking rules"), then out to your PMS diary and notes, Stripe payments, your team and a live
dashboard. A patient card shows one history across every channel.

> Patients reach me by phone, WhatsApp, SMS, email or web chat. I keep one history for every patient, follow
> your booking rules, update your PMS and take payments through Stripe.

**4. MONDAY 8:00: the call nobody was there for (0:48-1:24).** Back to that ringing phone. This time it's
answered. Live call with transcript; the diary fills; cards: Registered · Added to your diary · Deposit taken
(Stripe) · Summary and next action in notes. Counter 1.

> Adele (call): Good morning, Riverside Dental, this is Adele. How can I help?
> Patient (call): Hi, I've just moved to the area. Can I register and book a check-up?
> Adele (call): Of course. I have Wednesday at nine thirty. Shall I book that?
> Patient (call): Yes please.
> Adele (call): Done. I'll send you a secure link for the deposit, with directions and parking.
>
> Narration: Registered, booked, and the deposit taken, before reception opens.

**5. MONDAY 8:30: the team walks in (1:24-1:50).** Lights up in reception. The practice manager opens the
Adele portal. "Since Friday": the appointments Adele booked over the weekend and this morning, each with its
channel (phone, WhatsApp, web chat) and a one-line summary; deposits taken. At the top, "Needs your team": two
flagged items, for example "Treatment question about implants · needs a clinician" and "Asked to speak to the
practice manager". Counter jumps to the weekend total.

> At half past eight, your team walks in to a portal that's already up to date: the appointments I booked over
> the weekend and this morning, with a summary of every conversation. Anything that needs a person is flagged
> at the top, so they start the day knowing exactly what's theirs. Anything urgent, I pass to them straight away.

**6. MONDAY 9:12: WhatsApp first (1:50-2:16).** A website enquiry lands while the team is busy; within minutes
Adele follows up on WhatsApp, and the brochure's exchange books Thursday 4:15 with a deposit. Then side by
side: a long reminder by SMS, split into three charged parts, and the same reminder on WhatsApp as one message
with a map link and Yes / No buttons. Counter +1.

> A website enquiry comes in while everyone's busy. I follow it up within minutes, on WhatsApp, where your
> patients already are. I offer times, book the one they choose, and take the deposit. And for reminders, it
> costs less than text: a long SMS is charged in parts, but on WhatsApp it's one message, with directions and
> a one-tap reply.

**7. TUESDAY 13:05: a 2pm cancellation (2:16-2:36).** The 2pm slot turns red. Adele offers it on WhatsApp to the
waitlist you choose (tabs: Private · Kids); Sarah replies "Yes please"; the slot turns blue. Counter +1.

> Tuesday. A two o'clock cancels an hour before. I offer the slot to the waitlist you've chosen, and the first
> patient to say yes is moved in. The chair stays full.

**8. WEDNESDAY 7:40: the hygienist is off sick (2:36-2:52).** Eight appointments in the day's list; one by one
each is offered a new time and moves. Counter: "+8 appointments kept".

> Wednesday. Your hygienist is off sick. Before the first patient leaves home, I've moved every one of them to
> a new time.

**9. THURSDAY: the patient who hasn't confirmed (2:52-3:22).** The four steps from the brochure light up in
turn: When booked (reminder, directions, parking) · 2 days before (WhatsApp Yes / No, SMS optional) · 1 day
before (automated call) · Still no reply (flagged to your team). Friday's 9:30 patient walks the path and lands
in the portal's "Needs your team" list. A missed visit is followed up and rebooked.

> Every appointment is confirmed. When it's booked, I send a reminder with directions and parking. Two days
> before, I ask on WhatsApp: yes or no? No reply, and I call the day before. Still nothing, and it's flagged to
> your team. If someone does miss their visit, I follow up and rebook them, under your own DNA policy.

**10. FRIDAY: recall (3:22-3:46).** "64 patients overdue for hygiene." Messages go out on WhatsApp, by call and
by email; bookings tick in; the counter climbs. A completed hygiene visit triggers a whitening suggestion. A
lapsed patient gets the offer you set.

> Friday is recall day, and recall is where most of your revenue is waiting. Patients overdue for check-ups
> and hygiene hear from me on WhatsApp, by call and by email. After a hygiene visit, I can suggest treatments
> like whitening, and lapsed patients are invited back with the offers you set.

## Act 3: what the week was worth (3:46-4:30)

**11. FRIDAY 17:00: the weekly report (3:46-4:04).** The owner's dashboard opens: New patient enquiries by

**12. You stay in control (4:04-4:16).** Toggles flip: Missed enquiry follow-up (on) · Recall campaigns (on) ·

> And you're always in control. Every task runs automatically or manually, your choice, and I track your lab

**13. Built to be trusted (4:16-4:24).** Checklist: Call recording you control · No clinical outcome data
collected · Follows your rules and policies. Then a card marked "Planned": Full NHS compliance ·
Cybersecurity protocols.

> Call recording is yours to control, and I never collect clinical outcome data. We're working towards full

**14. Invitation (4:24-4:36).** The reception from the opening, now calm; the logo builds; "Pilot places from
December 2026 are limited"; adelecare.co.uk.

> See me on a real practice day. Pilot places from December 2026 are limited. Apply at adelecare dot co dot uk.

## Accuracy notes

- Capabilities shown, all from the brochure or prototype spec: inbound calls with urgent and overflow calls
  passed to staff; call summary and next action written to notes; booking, cancelling and rescheduling in
  the PMS; payments through Stripe; one portal with history across channels; staff notified of what needs a
  person; new patient registration; reminders with directions and parking; two-step confirmation (WhatsApp 2
  days before, automated call 1 day before, flagged if no reply); waitlists chosen by the practice, with
  groups such as kids and private; rescheduling when a clinician is off; DNA follow-up under practice rules;
  missed enquiries followed up within minutes; recall by call, WhatsApp and email with a detailed report;
  treatment suggestions such as whitening after hygiene; offers for lapsed patients; automatic or manual per
  task; dashboard of enquiries, chase success, conversions and revenue; lab work tracking.
- WhatsApp cost claim is limited to reminders. Meta charges per message by category: in the UK a "utility"
  message (reminders, confirmations) is about 1.6p, replies inside a 24-hour customer-service window are free,
  and a "marketing" message (promotional recall offers) is about 4-5p, similar to an SMS. An SMS over 160
  characters is billed as several segments. So the film says WhatsApp is cheaper for reminders, not for every
  message. Check current rates on Meta's rate card before quoting figures to a practice.
- "Your PMS" throughout; Dentally is not named. Stripe is named.
- Compliance is a plan ("working towards", "Planned"), never presented as certification.
- Riverside Dental, Sarah, times and counts are illustrative. Phone numbers use Ofcom's 07700 900xxx drama range.
